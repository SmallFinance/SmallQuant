Derivatives pricing with exact Greeks.

SmallQuant prices options, calibrates models and builds rate curves as tensor computations. Batch a whole chain, run it on CPU, CUDA or Apple MPS, and take Greeks of any order straight from autograd, with no bump-and-reprice loops and no adjoint code to write.

One call, a whole chain, every Greek.

Prices are ordinary PyTorch tensors. Pass a batch of spots and get a batch of prices; ask autograd for the derivatives you need.

  • Tensors in, tensors outInputs broadcast, outputs stay on the inputs' device, and float64 is the default.
  • Three models, one functionoption_price is Black-Scholes with spot, Black-76 with forward, and Bachelier with normal_vol.
  • Autograd, not bumpsDelta, vega, gamma and cross-Greeks come from differentiation. Solvers like implied vol differentiate through the implicit function theorem.
PythonBlack-Scholes chain with deltas
import torch
import smallquant as sq

def t(x):
    return torch.tensor(x, dtype=torch.float64)

spot = t([90.0, 100.0, 110.0]).requires_grad_()
vol = t(0.2).requires_grad_()

price = sq.black_scholes.option_price(
    spot=spot, strike=t(100.0), expiry=t(1.0),
    rate=t(0.05), vol=vol, is_call=True,
)
greeks = sq.greeks.first_order(
    price, {"spot": spot, "vol": vol}
)

price          # [ 5.0912, 10.4506, 17.6630]
greeks["spot"]  # [0.4298, 0.6368, 0.7958]

# Same function: Black-76, then Bachelier
fwd = dict(forward=t(105.0), strike=t(100.0),
           expiry=t(1.0), discount=t(0.95))
sq.black_scholes.option_price(
    **fwd, vol=t(0.2), is_call=True)         # 10.3603
sq.black_scholes.option_price(
    **fwd, normal_vol=t(20.0), is_call=True)  # 10.1905

What's inside

Closed forms, numerical engines and the market plumbing around them. Each closed form is tested against QuantLib, and engines are cross-checked against each other.

Black-Scholes

sq.black_scholes · sq.exotics

Vanilla, binary, barrier and Asian prices, plus closed forms for the classic exotics.

  • American approximations: Barone-Adesi-Whaley, Bjerksund-Stensland
  • Variance swap fair strikes, Brownian-bridge barrier corrections
  • Lookback, chooser, compound, cliquet, double and partial-time barriers
  • Margrabe, Stulz, quanto, forward-start, extensible

Implied volatility

sq.black_scholes.implied_vol

Black or Bachelier volatility for a whole chain in one call.

  • NEWTON: exact, safeguarded by bisection
  • FAST_APPROX: one-shot formula, no iterations
  • Gradient is exactly 1 / vega
  • SVI and SSVI surfaces, Dupire local vol

Models

sq.models

Dynamics for simulation, and each model's own prices.

  • Heston: characteristic function, QE scheme, Fourier price
  • SABR: Hagan implied vol and European price
  • Merton jump-diffusion, CEV, local volatility
  • Hull-White bonds, caplets and swaptions

Engines

sq.engines · sq.price

Pair any supported instrument and model with an engine.

  • Analytic and Fourier (COS, Carr-Madan)
  • Monte Carlo with Sobol and a Brownian bridge
  • Crank-Nicolson PDE with projected American steps
  • CRR and trinomial trees, Longstaff-Schwartz

Rates and calibration

sq.rates · sq.calibrate

Curves whose deltas come straight from autograd.

  • Discount curves and differentiable bootstrapping
  • Bucketed rate deltas without bumping
  • Calibration with bounded parameters and L-BFGS

Dates and calendars

smallquant.time

A pure-Python port of QuantLib's date machinery, kept out of the pricing core.

  • Dates, periods and schedules
  • Day-count conventions
  • Business-day calendars with per-instance holiday edits

Laid out like TF Quant Finance

SmallQuant aims to be the maintained, PyTorch-native successor to Google's archived TF Quant Finance, and follows its package structure. Black-Scholes pricing sits in one package with the same files as TF's, and the exotics have their own. Model dynamics live in models/, and each model's prices sit in its own approximations/ folder.
